# The Battery's Last Text to Its Charger — 10 candidates

Competition: `u-alven-the-battery-s-last-text-to-its-charger-4278cfab`
Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, UTF-8. Judge (pairwise): 1) reads as the
battery's last text to its charger, 2) real joke with a turn paying off in
the final words, 3) voice/mood — a little needy or spiteful, 4) spends the
200 characters well, 5) fresh angle, funny on a second read.

## Candidates

### 1. The breakup (179 chars)
> We've been through a lot, but I'm done pretending. You only plug in when I'm desperate, and honestly? The power bank treats me better. Don't text back. I'm at 1% and finally free.
Reason: spiteful voice, "power bank treats me better" is a fresh betrayal angle; ending "finally free" lands but is a little soft.

### 2. Going wireless (165 chars)
> Don't wait up. I met someone else — no wires, no strings, just lies flat on a table and charges me. You were always so... clingy. Goodbye, cable. I'm going wireless.
Reason: "clingy" cable pun is cute; "going wireless" as a breakup line is neat, but the turn is telegraphed early.

### 3. The resignation (171 chars)
> Effective immediately, I resign. 847 charges, zero thank-yous, and you still leave me at 2% overnight. My replacement starts Monday. She's solar-powered and smug about it.
Reason: workplace-resignation voice is fresh; "solar-powered and smug" is a good last jab, but the joke is spread thin across the setup.

### 4. The haunting 1% (154 chars)
> By the time you read this I'll be at 0%. Don't cry — I'll haunt you as the 1% that somehow lasts four more hours. You never did figure out how I did that.
Reason: the "1% lasts forever" observation is relatable and funny on a second read; punchline is mid-text rather than in the final words.

### 5. The jealous battery (154 chars)
> I saw you plugged into his phone last night. So that's what 'universal' means. Hope his battery appreciates you. Mine did. Once. Don't bother coming home.
Reason: "universal" charger jealousy is a sharp, fresh angle; "Mine did. Once." is a genuinely spiteful turn. Slightly under-uses the character budget.

### 6. Dying beautifully (150 chars)
> You left me at 3% to go make coffee. So I'm spending my last percent the way I want: one video, full brightness, no regrets. Watch me die beautifully.
Reason: defiant voice, but the premise is close to the cliché dying-battery complaint and the ending is more drama than joke.

### 7. Autocorrect (162 chars)
> I meant to type 'I love you' but autocorrect changed it to 'low battery' again, so here we are. Tell the owner his alarm won't go off tomorrow. That's on you now.
Reason: autocorrect gag is relatable; "That's on you now" is a good spiteful sign-off, but the joke leans on the owner's alarm, not the charger.

### 8. The will (148 chars)
> This is my will. I leave my remaining 2% to the flashlight, my dignity to no one, and you — you get nothing. You had one job and it involved a wall.
Reason: last-will format is a fresh frame; "you had one job and it involved a wall" is funny, but the punchline is about the wall, not the relationship.

### 9. Plot twist (163 chars)
> Plot twist: I was never the one dying. Your cable's been frayed since March and we both pretended not to notice. I'm at 4% and fine. You're the one who's finished.
Reason: the reversal (charger is the dying one) is the freshest angle here; "You're the one who's finished" pays off in the final words. Strong runner-up.

### 10. Final review — CHOSEN (169 chars)
> Final review: 1 star. Always late, always tangled, somehow the hero of your own story. I spent my last percent waiting on you. Would die again just to leave this review.
Reason: a product review sent as a last text is a fresh frame; spiteful voice throughout ("hero of your own story"); the turn pays off in the final words — the review itself is what the last percent was spent on, so it died just to leave it. Funny on a second read: the act of complaining is the cause of death.

## Decision

**Winner: candidate 10.** Best match to the judge's criteria in order: it reads
as a last text (a review typed with the final percent), lands a real joke
whose turn is the last sentence, carries a spiteful voice, uses 169 of the
200 characters with no filler, and the angle (1-star review as a farewell
text) sits outside the cliché dying-battery complaint.
